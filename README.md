## Overview

- Brief overview                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
The purpose of this dataset is to analyze user experience in a virtual reality (VR) environment, focusing on a comparative study between visual and audiovisual stimuli based on the archaeological site of Edzna, Mexico. The immersive experience allowed participants to explore the site without needing to physically being there, and the experiment was conducted in a museum setting, offering a unique experience that goes beyond traditional visual-only exhibits. The dataset includes both Electroencephalography (EEG) recordings from eight channels (Fz, C3, Cz, C4, Pz, PO7, Oz, and PO8) and user responses to the User Experience Questionnaire (UEQ), providing necessary data for future studies on how immersive environments affect user perception. 
The EEG data was collected using a Unicorn Hybrid Black EEG system with a sampling rate of 250 Hz. Participants were exposed to two conditions: a visual-only stimulus and an audiovisual stimulus, both of which represented scenes from the archaeological site in VR. Prior to exposure, a baseline measurement was taken to capture the initial state of the participants. Data collection was conducted in MOSTLA, a digital innovation lab at Tecnologico de Monterrey campus, and the Museum of Contemporary Art in Monterrey.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
Each EEG recording is shared in .set format and follows the BIDS structure. The recordings include eight channels of brainwave recordings for the baseline, visual, and audiovisual conditions. The signals are presented in both formats: raw and preprocessed. Additionally, an .xlsx file is provided with basic participant metadata, such as age, gender, unique identifier as well as the UEQ responses.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
Each EEG file contains data segmented into the three phases of the experiment: baseline, visual stimulus, and audiovisual stimulus, allowing researchers to directly compare neural responses across conditions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
This dataset offers a comprehensive resource for researchers interested in investigating the effects of immersive VR environments on user engagement, and attention, making it highly applicable and useful. ## NEMAR curation changes (2026-05-21)

BIDS validator: 1 error + 7039 warnings -> 0 errors + 3366 warnings. Raw `.set` binary payloads unchanged.

### `task-Edzna_events.json` (root sidecar — was empty array)
- Rewrote the file from the literal `[]` (empty JSON array) it shipped with into a proper JSON object documenting the `sample` and `value` columns that actually appear in every per-recording `_events.tsv`. Why: BIDS requires sidecar JSON files to be objects, not arrays; the empty-array shape fired the only `JSON_NOT_AN_OBJECT` error and also left the optional TSV columns `sample` + `value` undeclared (`TSV_ADDITIONAL_COLUMNS_UNDEFINED` × 153 each).
- The column descriptions identify these rows as EEGLAB boundary annotations marking joins between the three concatenated experiment phases (baseline / visual / audiovisual) carried over from the source `.set` files, which is how every populated `_events.tsv` in this dataset is actually shaped (all rows have `value = "boundary"`).

### `task-Edzna_eeg.json` (new — inheriting root sidecar)
- Created a single root sidecar carrying every BIDS-EEG field that is invariant across the 306 per-recording sidecars (`TaskName`, `SamplingFrequency`, `PowerLineFrequency`, `EEGReference`, `SoftwareFilters`, `RecordingType`, `EEGChannelCount`, `EEGPlacementScheme`, `CapManufacturer`, `CapManufacturersModelName`, `InstitutionName`, `InstitutionAddress`). Why: BIDS-inheritance broadcasts these from a root sidecar to every recording, so the per-recording sidecars no longer need to repeat them. Closes the cohort-wide recommended-key warnings in one place rather than 306 places.
- Added `Manufacturer: "g.tec medical engineering"` and `ManufacturersModelName: "Unicorn Hybrid Black"`. Why: the README explicitly identifies the recording hardware as a "Unicorn Hybrid Black EEG system" and the per-recording sidecars already carried `CapManufacturer: "G-Tech"` + `CapManufacturersModelName: "Unicorn Hybrid Black"` for the same integrated cap-plus-amplifier unit; `Manufacturer`/`ManufacturersModelName` are the corresponding BIDS-canonical keys for the amplifier side of that one device.
- Added `EOGChannelCount`, `ECGChannelCount`, `EMGChannelCount`, `MISCChannelCount`, `TriggerChannelCount` all set to `0`. Why: every `_channels.tsv` body lists exactly the 8 scalp EEG electrodes and no rows of any other channel type, so each of these counts is mechanically verifiable as zero.
- Added `TaskDescription`. Why: BIDS recommends a free-form description of the task; the value here paraphrases the README's task description (continuous recording containing concatenated baseline / visual-stimulus / audiovisual-stimulus phases inside the Edzna VR exhibit) without adding any detail the README does not state.

### `sub-*/eeg/sub-*_task-Edzna_run-*_eeg.json` (per-recording sidecars — slimmed)
- Each of the 306 per-recording `_eeg.json` files now contains only the per-recording `RecordingDuration` value. Why: every other field these files used to carry was identical across the cohort and now lives in `task-Edzna_eeg.json`, so leaving the duplicates in place would only obscure where each field is defined.

### `sub-*/eeg/sub-*_task-Edzna_run-*_channels.tsv` (306 files)
- Set `type = EEG` and `units = uV` on every channel row (the eight rows in each file all hold standard scalp-EEG electrodes: `Fz, P3 or C3, Cz, P4 or C4, Pz, PO7, Oz, PO8`). Why: these rows shipped as `type = n/a, units = n/a`, which produced the 306× `EEG_CHANNEL_COUNT_MISMATCH` warning because the validator could not count any `type = EEG` rows even though the paired sidecar declared `EEGChannelCount: 8`. The Unicorn Hybrid Black system records scalp EEG in microvolts by default, which matches the bandpass `1-50Hz` filter description the dataset already carries in `SoftwareFilters`.
- Channel-name labels were left untouched. The raw `sub-N` recordings use `P3`/`P4`; the preprocessed `sub-Np` recordings relabel those two channels as `C3`/`C4`. Both labels denote standard scalp-EEG electrode positions, so the `type = EEG, units = uV` assignment is correct in both cases; reconciling the relabel is a content question for the original authors, not a mechanical sidecar fix.

### `participants.json`
- Expanded the per-column `Description` entries. Why: the previous one-line descriptions ("Unique participant label", "Participant group label") fired `SIDECAR_KEY_RECOMMENDED:Description` because they did not describe the dataset-specific convention (the `*p` suffix indicating preprocessed counterparts) or acknowledge what the source documents about `Group` (only its name; the per-group semantics are not stated in the source). Rewrote the descriptions to make those facts explicit without inventing information that the source does not state.

### `dataset_description.json`
- Added `DatasetType: "raw"`. Why: BIDS-validator falls back to derivative-dataset rules when `DatasetType` is missing alongside `GeneratedBy`, producing a cascade of spurious derivative-specific warnings; the dataset is clearly raw (top-level `sub-*/eeg/` layout, no `derivatives/`).
- Added a `nemar-cli` entry to `GeneratedBy`. Why: documents the rehost provenance alongside the existing `bids-matlab-tools` entry that produced the original BIDS conversion.
- Replaced `ReferencesAndLinks: [""]` (a single empty-string element) with `ReferencesAndLinks: []`. Why: the empty-string element does not satisfy the array-of-URL schema and carries no information.

### Tier-C warnings deliberately left in place
The validator still surfaces ~3366 recommended-key warnings. They are not fixed here because the source dataset does not document the required values and inventing them would be unsafe:
- Equipment / institution: `SoftwareVersions`, `DeviceSerialNumber`, `InstitutionalDepartmentName`.
- Method: `EEGGround` (where on the head the reference/ground electrode was placed), `HardwareFilters` (amplifier-side filters distinct from the OpenVibe software bandpass already documented), `HeadCircumference` (per subject).
- Annotation: `SubjectArtefactDescription`, `Instructions`, `StimulusPresentation` (the README documents only "VR Headset" + "Headphones" + two YouTube URLs, which is insufficient to fill the BIDS-canonical `StimulusPresentation` object with OS / SoftwareName / SoftwareVersion / Code).
- Ontology: `CogAtlasID`, `CogPOID` — require judgment about ontology mapping that the source does not state.

The remaining 153× `EVENTS_TSV_MISSING` warnings fire on the raw `sub-N` recordings, which ship without `_events.tsv` files. The raw `.set` files carry no EEGLAB annotations (`n_annotations = 0` for every recording in the load-time baseline), so there is no defensible mechanical regeneration; the preprocessed `sub-Np` counterparts do carry `_events.tsv` files (boundary-only) since EEGLAB inserts boundary markers during preprocessing.
