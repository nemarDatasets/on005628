## Overview

- Brief overview                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
The purpose of this dataset is to analyze user experience in a virtual reality (VR) environment, focusing on a comparative study between visual and audiovisual stimuli based on the archaeological site of Edzna, Mexico. The immersive experience allowed participants to explore the site without needing to physically being there, and the experiment was conducted in a museum setting, offering a unique experience that goes beyond traditional visual-only exhibits. The dataset includes both Electroencephalography (EEG) recordings from eight channels (Fz, C3, Cz, C4, Pz, PO7, Oz, and PO8) and user responses to the User Experience Questionnaire (UEQ), providing necessary data for future studies on how immersive environments affect user perception. 
The EEG data was collected using a Unicorn Hybrid Black EEG system with a sampling rate of 250 Hz. Participants were exposed to two conditions: a visual-only stimulus and an audiovisual stimulus, both of which represented scenes from the archaeological site in VR. Prior to exposure, a baseline measurement was taken to capture the initial state of the participants. Data collection was conducted in MOSTLA, a digital innovation lab at Tecnologico de Monterrey campus, and the Museum of Contemporary Art in Monterrey.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
Each EEG recording is shared in .set format and follows the BIDS structure. The recordings include eight channels of brainwave recordings for the baseline, visual, and audiovisual conditions. The signals are presented in both formats: raw and preprocessed. Additionally, an .xlsx file is provided with basic participant metadata, such as age, gender, unique identifier as well as the UEQ responses.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
Each EEG file contains data segmented into the three phases of the experiment: baseline, visual stimulus, and audiovisual stimulus, allowing researchers to directly compare neural responses across conditions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            
This dataset offers a comprehensive resource for researchers interested in investigating the effects of immersive VR environments on user engagement, and attention, making it highly applicable and useful. ## NEMAR curation changes (2026-05-21, revised 2026-05-27)

The BIDS validator went from 1 error + 7039 warnings to 0 errors + 3366 warnings. None of the raw `.set` files were modified — every change is to a text sidecar.

**Root events sidecar (`task-Edzna_events.json`)**
- The file shipped as the literal `[]` (an empty JSON array). BIDS requires sidecar JSON files to be objects, not arrays, so the empty-array shape was the only validator error. Rewrote it as a proper object that documents the `sample` and `value` columns that actually appear in every per-recording `_events.tsv` — this also clears the "additional column undefined" warning those columns were firing.
- The descriptions identify the rows as EEGLAB boundary annotations marking the joins between the three concatenated experiment phases (baseline, visual, audiovisual) carried over from the source `.set` files. Every populated `_events.tsv` in the dataset is in fact boundary-only (all rows have `value = "boundary"`), so this description matches what is actually on disk.

**New shared EEG sidecar at the dataset root (`task-Edzna_eeg.json`)** (so the same information isn't repeated in all 306 recording sidecars; BIDS applies a root sidecar to every matching recording)
- Records the fields that are identical for every recording: task name, sampling frequency, line frequency, reference, software filters, recording type, EEG channel count, placement scheme, cap manufacturer and model, institution name, and institution address. These used to be duplicated across all 306 per-recording sidecars.
- Adds `Manufacturer: "g.tec medical engineering"` and `ManufacturersModelName: "Unicorn Hybrid Black"`. The README explicitly names the hardware as a "Unicorn Hybrid Black EEG system", and the per-recording sidecars already named the same integrated cap-plus-amplifier unit under the cap-side keys; these are the matching amplifier-side BIDS keys for that one device.
- Sets `EOGChannelCount`, `ECGChannelCount`, `EMGChannelCount`, `MISCChannelCount`, and `TriggerChannelCount` to `0`. Every `_channels.tsv` body lists exactly the 8 scalp EEG electrodes and no rows of any other type, so each of these counts is mechanically verifiable from the data.
- Adds a `TaskDescription` that paraphrases the README's own task description (a continuous recording containing the concatenated baseline, visual-stimulus, and audiovisual-stimulus phases inside the Edzna VR exhibit) without adding any detail the README does not state.

**Per-recording EEG sidecars (`sub-*/eeg/sub-*_task-Edzna_run-*_eeg.json`, 306 files) — slimmed**
- Each per-recording sidecar now carries only its own `RecordingDuration`. Every other field these files used to repeat is identical across the cohort and now lives in the shared root sidecar above, so keeping the duplicates would only obscure where each field is actually defined.

**Channel tables (`sub-*/eeg/sub-*_task-Edzna_run-*_channels.tsv`, 306 files)**
- Each file lists eight rows for standard scalp-EEG electrodes (`Fz`, `P3` or `C3`, `Cz`, `P4` or `C4`, `Pz`, `PO7`, `Oz`, `PO8`); they all shipped with `type = n/a, units = n/a`. The paired sidecar declares `EEGChannelCount: 8`, but with no `type = EEG` rows the validator could not see those eight channels and warned about a channel-count mismatch on every file. Set `type = EEG` and `units = uV` on every channel row — the Unicorn Hybrid Black records scalp EEG in microvolts by default, consistent with the `1–50 Hz` bandpass already documented in `SoftwareFilters`.
- Channel-name labels were left exactly as published. The raw `sub-N` recordings use `P3`/`P4`; the preprocessed `sub-Np` recordings relabel those two as `C3`/`C4`. Both labels are standard scalp positions, so the `type = EEG, units = uV` assignment is correct either way; reconciling the relabel is a content question for the original authors, not a mechanical sidecar fix.

**Participants sidecar (`participants.json`)**
- Expanded the per-column descriptions. The previous one-line texts ("Unique participant label", "Participant group label") were too thin to satisfy the recommended-description check and, more usefully, did not mention the dataset-specific `*p` suffix that marks preprocessed counterparts or acknowledge that the source names the `Group` column but does not document what the groups mean. Rewrote them to make those facts explicit without inventing anything the source does not say.

**Dataset description (`dataset_description.json`)**
- Updated `BIDSVersion` from `1.8.0` to `1.11.1` (the version the current validator checks against).
- Added `DatasetType: "raw"`. With both `DatasetType` and `GeneratedBy` missing, the validator falls back to derivative-dataset rules and emits a cascade of derivative-specific warnings; this dataset is clearly raw (top-level `sub-*/eeg/` layout, no `derivatives/`).
- Replaced `ReferencesAndLinks: [""]` with an empty array. The single empty-string element does not satisfy the URL-array schema and carries no information.
- Kept the source's `GeneratedBy` entry (`bids-matlab-tools`, version 9.1) exactly as published; nothing else was added there. That field records the tooling that generated the data, not our rehost step.

**Remaining warnings (3366) — left on purpose**
- These are all "recommended but missing" fields that need information from the study, lab, or equipment that isn't in the dataset. They were left blank rather than filled with guesses:
  - Equipment and institution: `SoftwareVersions`, `DeviceSerialNumber`, `InstitutionalDepartmentName`.
  - Method: `EEGGround` (where on the head the ground electrode was placed), `HardwareFilters` (amplifier-side filters, distinct from the software bandpass already documented), `HeadCircumference` (per subject).
  - Annotation: `SubjectArtefactDescription`, `Instructions`, `StimulusPresentation` (the README documents only "VR Headset", "Headphones", and two YouTube URLs — not enough to fill the BIDS-canonical object's OS / software-name / software-version / code fields).
  - Ontology: `CogAtlasID`, `CogPOID` — require judgment about ontology mapping that the source does not state.
- The 153 "events file missing" warnings fire on the raw `sub-N` recordings, which ship without `_events.tsv` files. The raw `.set` files carry no EEGLAB annotations (zero annotations on every recording at load time), so there is no defensible mechanical regeneration; the preprocessed `sub-Np` counterparts do carry boundary-only `_events.tsv` files because EEGLAB inserts boundary markers during preprocessing.
